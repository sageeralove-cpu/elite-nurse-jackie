Day 1
Cortisol & Blood Sugar Reset
Light exposure + protein-first morning
0/5

Cortisol follows a natural diurnal rhythm — peaking 30–45 minutes after waking (the Cortisol Awakening Response). Shift work destroys this rhythm. Today anchors it through light and nutrition.

  • Expose yourself to bright light within 10 minutes of waking (natural or SAD lamp, 10,000 lux, 20 minutes).
  • Eat a protein-rich breakfast within 60 minutes of waking (25–30g protein: eggs, Greek yoghurt, smoked salmon).
  • Delay caffeine for 90 minutes after waking — let cortisol peak naturally first.
  • Eat every 3–4 hours to prevent reactive hypoglycaemia. No skipped meals today.
  • Rate your energy and mood at morning, midday and evening (1–10). This is your hormonal baseline.
🩺 Nurse Tip: On night shift, your 'morning' is whenever you wake. Apply the same rules: protein first, delay caffeine 90 minutes, open a window or step outside briefly.

Day 2
Sleep & Melatonin Optimisation
Building your hormonal sleep sanctuary
0/5

Blue light from screens suppresses melatonin by up to 85%. Chronic melatonin disruption in shift workers is associated with metabolic syndrome, depression, and cardiovascular disease. Today builds the foundations.

  • Create a sleep blackout environment: blackout curtains, eye mask, white noise or earplugs.
  • Begin screen-free wind-down 60 minutes before bed. Use blue-light blocking glasses from 9pm.
  • Consider 0.5–3mg melatonin 30 minutes before desired sleep time (consult your GP first).
  • Keep bedroom temperature between 16–18°C for optimal sleep onset.
  • Consider magnesium glycinate (200–400mg before bed) — supports sleep and progesterone (check with GP).
🩺 Nurse Tip: Post-night shift sleep is often the least restorative. Prioritise duration over timing — even 6 hours of true darkness is hormonal medicine.

Day 3
Gut Health & Oestrogen Metabolism
Feeding your estrobolome
0/5

The 'estrobolome' — gut bacteria responsible for metabolising oestrogen — is a critical regulator of hormonal balance. Poor gut health causes oestrogen to be reabsorbed rather than excreted, contributing to oestrogen dominance.

  • Eat 30g of dietary fibre today: vegetables, legumes, whole grains, 1 tbsp ground flaxseed (daily).
  • Add 1–2 servings of fermented foods: kefir, natural yoghurt, kimchi, sauerkraut, or kombucha.
  • Reduce or eliminate alcohol for this week — it directly impairs liver oestrogen detoxification.
  • Hydrate: aim for 2–2.5L of water. Add electrolytes on long shift days.
  • Consider a quality probiotic with Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium strains.
🩺 Nurse Tip: Stress-induced gut permeability ('leaky gut') accelerates hormonal disruption. Feed your gut bacteria on every day off.

Day 4
Movement for Hormonal Health
Zone 2 over HIIT
0/5

High-intensity exercise raises cortisol — damaging when overlaid on an already elevated baseline. Moderate Zone 2 cardio (60–70% max HR) improves insulin sensitivity, reduces cortisol, and supports thyroid function.

  • 30 minutes of Zone 2 cardio: brisk walking, light cycling, swimming (you can hold a conversation).
  • If energy allows: 2 sets of bodyweight strength exercises — squats, lunges, push-ups (10–15 reps each).
  • Avoid HIIT this week if you're in a high-stress period — it will raise cortisol further.
  • Stretch for 10 minutes in the evening. Yoga nidra (20-min guided recording) is particularly effective.
  • Walk outside for at least 20 minutes — natural light + gentle movement = powerful hormonal combination.
🩺 Nurse Tip: A 12-hour night shift is physical exercise. On shift days, gentle walking and stretching are enough. Save more intensive movement for your days off.

Day 5
Key Nutrients for Hormones
Zinc, selenium, Vitamin D, omega-3
0/5

Specific micronutrient deficiencies directly impair hormone production. Nurses are at significant risk due to irregular meals, high stress, and frequent illness exposure.

  • Prioritise zinc-rich foods: pumpkin seeds, beef, oysters, chickpeas, cashews.
  • Eat 2 Brazil nuts daily for selenium (thyroid support) — do not exceed 4 per day.
  • Check your Vitamin D level with your GP. Most people require supplementation (1000–4000 IU/day).
  • Add omega-3 rich foods: oily fish 2–3 times this week, or fish oil supplement (2g EPA+DHA/day).
  • Reduce refined sugar and ultra-processed foods — they drive insulin spikes, inflammation, and cortisol.
🩺 Nurse Tip: Meal prepping on days off — even just boiled eggs, mixed nuts, and chopped veg — transforms your nutritional baseline on shift.

Day 6
Stress Response & Adrenal Support
Parasympathetic activation + adaptogenic herbs
0/5

Chronic HPA axis activation depletes adrenal reserves, resulting in a flattened cortisol curve, fatigue, and hormonal cascade failure. Adaptogenic herbs have been shown in RCTs to modulate cortisol response.

  • Ashwagandha (KSM-66, 300–600mg): shown to reduce cortisol by up to 27.9% in RCTs. Consult GP first.
  • Practice 4-7-8 breathing (in 4, hold 7, out 8) for 5 minutes — directly activates the vagus nerve.
  • Schedule at least 2 hours of unstructured restorative time today — no obligations, no screens.
  • Eat regularly. Skipping meals spikes cortisol. Three balanced meals: protein, fat, complex carbohydrate.
  • Limit caffeine to before noon and to maximum 2 cups.
🩺 Nurse Tip: You cannot pour from an empty cup — and neither can your adrenals. Treating rest as non-negotiable is clinical practice, not self-indulgence.

Day 7
Circadian Reset & Consolidation
Sustainable hormonal anchoring
0/5

The circadian clock governs the timing of virtually every hormone. Shift work creates 'circadian misalignment' — associated with metabolic syndrome, reproductive disruption, and cardiovascular disease.

  • Set consistent sleep and wake times even on days off — the single most powerful circadian anchor.
  • Time-restricted eating: all meals within a 10–12 hour window (e.g. 7am–7pm).
  • Review Days 1–6: which practices had the most noticeable effect on your energy and mood?
  • Create your personalised Hormonal Reset Maintenance Plan — select 3 daily non-negotiables.
  • Book a baseline hormone panel with your GP: cortisol, DHEA-S, TSH/T3/T4, Vitamin D, ferritin, B12.
🩺 Nurse Tip: Hormonal rebalancing is not a one-week fix — it is a months-long process. This week gave you the foundation.
