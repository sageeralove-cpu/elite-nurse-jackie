Day 1
Awareness Without Judgement
Cognitive defusion + thought watching
0/4

Today is about noticing your thoughts like passing clouds — not engaging, not fighting. Simply label each one: 'I notice I'm having the thought that...' This single practice reduces amygdala reactivity within seconds.

  • Sit quietly for 5 minutes. Watch your thoughts — don't engage, just observe.
  • Each time a negative thought arises, say silently: 'I notice I'm having the thought that...'
  • Evening journal (10 min): Write your 3 most frequent negative thought themes.
  • Read your entries with curiosity, not self-criticism.
🩺 Clinical Tip: After a brutal shift, your brain replays every difficult moment. Defuse from them: 'My brain is trying to protect me. I can thank it and let it go.'

Day 2
Gratitude & Neuroplasticity
Rewiring attentional bias toward the positive
0/4

Gratitude activates the medial prefrontal cortex and releases dopamine and serotonin — creating self-reinforcing positive neural pathways. This is about specificity: not 'my health' but 'the way my colleague brought me tea without being asked'.

  • Write 3 specific gratitudes — granular and concrete, not generic.
  • Send a 2-sentence thank-you message to one colleague or person in your life.
  • Notice 5 sensory pleasures throughout your day (coffee, morning light, a comfortable chair).
  • Evening: What went right today, even minimally?
🩺 Clinical Tip: Gratitude on night shift can feel hollow. Try 'micro-gratitudes' — the peaceful handover, a moment to sit. Small moments count enormously.

Day 3
Values Clarification
Reconnecting with your purpose
0/4

ACT research shows psychological flexibility — acting in alignment with your values even under distress — is the strongest predictor of burnout prevention in healthcare. Today reconnects you to why you do what you do.

  • Values inventory (15 min): Choose your top 5 from: compassion, growth, family, freedom, integrity, creativity, service, courage, health, connection.
  • For each value, write: 'This value matters to me because...'
  • Identify one area most aligned with your values — and one most disconnected.
  • Write a 3-sentence personal mission statement.
🩺 Clinical Tip: Moral distress arises when you're forced to act against your values. Naming them gives language to that pain — the first step to addressing it.

Day 4
Self-Compassion
Turning care inward
0/4

Dr Kristin Neff's research: self-compassion is strongly associated with reduced burnout and increased resilience. Nurses score exceptionally low on self-compassion despite high scores for others. Today that changes.

  • Self-compassion break (5 min): Hand on heart — 'This is a moment of suffering. Suffering is a part of life. May I be kind to myself in this moment.'
  • Write a letter to yourself from the perspective of a deeply caring, wise friend.
  • Identify your harshest inner critic phrase and rewrite it with compassion.
  • Practice a 'self-compassion pause' each time shame or self-criticism arises.
🩺 Clinical Tip: Perfectionism keeps patients safe — but applied to yourself, it is corrosive. You are not your worst shift.

Day 5
Cognitive Reframing
CBT core — accurate thinking, not positive thinking
0/5

Identify cognitive distortions — catastrophising, mind-reading, all-or-nothing thinking — and replace them with accurate, balanced perspectives. This is not toxic positivity. It is precision thinking.

  • List your top 3 recurring negative thoughts from Day 1.
  • Identify the distortion type for each (catastrophising, personalising, mind-reading...).
  • Apply the ABCDE model: Adversity → Belief → Consequence → Dispute → new Effect.
  • Write 3 reframe statements — honest, balanced alternatives.
  • Catch and reframe one thought in real time today.
🩺 Clinical Tip: Common healthcare distortion: 'If I miss one thing, something terrible will happen.' Reframe: 'I work in a team with multiple safety nets. I am thorough and supported.'

Day 6
Intention Setting & Visualisation
Creating an internal locus of control
0/4

Mental rehearsal activates the same neural pathways as physical action. Setting clear daily intentions — rather than reacting to whatever the shift brings — creates an internal locus of control that buffers against stress.

  • Morning intention (3 min): 'Today, I intend to bring _____ to my work. I will honour my own needs by _____.'
  • Visualise (5 min): See yourself moving through a difficult moment today with calm competence.
  • Identify one boundary you want to set or reinforce this week.
  • Create a simple shutdown ritual: 3 deep breaths, wash hands intentionally, say 'I have done enough for today.'
🩺 Clinical Tip: Try intention setting in the car before entering the building. It takes 90 seconds and changes everything about how you enter.

Day 7
Integration & Commitment
Building your permanent practice
0/5

New neural pathways require approximately 60–90 days of practice to become default. Today you design your maintenance protocol — making these shifts part of who you are, not just a 7-day experiment.

  • Review your journals from Days 1–6. What has shifted?
  • Select 2–3 practices that resonated most — these become your daily anchors.
  • Write a 'Future Self' letter: Who are you in 6 months if you keep this up?
  • Share this guide with one colleague who needs it.
  • Schedule your next 7-Day Mindset Reset — one month from today.
🩺 Clinical Tip: Real change is non-linear. There will be hard shifts and days when none of this is accessible. That is not failure — it is being human and working in healthcare.
